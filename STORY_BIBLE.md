# WOA: Arcane Realm — Story Bible

## The premise
The Discord Arcane Realm is the community's mythic record of the wider world: sigils are earned through deeds, riddles guard old knowledge, and the Council maintains the balance between realms. The game turns that record into a living dark-fantasy adventure.

## Opening chapter: Gloamwick
The apprentice arrives in a small town under a violet, failing sky. Three riftbound attackers are assaulting Mathias, a Battle Mage of the Council of Wizards, Warlocks & Witches. Helping him is the player's first meaningful choice and the first proof that they belong in the Realm.

## Mathias and the first mystery
Mathias explains that the ward-stones have been corrupted across the realms. The Council's records, old riddles, realm trials, relics, and bounty ledgers all point to a coordinated threat. He guides the apprentice toward the Sigil Sanctuary and its three seals: Dawn, Dusk, and Heart.

## How the Discord bot's lore becomes game lore
- **Council panel and High Wizards:** the Council is the in-world keeper of realm law, history, and major decisions.
- **Arcane Realm roles and profiles:** wizard paths, ranks, XP, health, mana, and earned sigils become character progression.
- **Sigils and events:** community deeds and realm events become the historical source of sigil fragments and rewards.
- **Riddles and the Codex:** riddles protect knowledge, while the Codex records enemies, items, spells, relics, and realm history.
- **Bounties and Trials:** bounties are dangerous quests issued by the realm's keepers; Trials test a champion's skill and judgment.
- **Grand Sigil Exchange:** the in-world exchange preserves relics, spells, cosmetics, and other earned rewards. It is a lore institution, not a real-money shop.
- **PvE and PvP champions:** friendly rivalries and combat trials are different ways for characters to prove themselves; the main story remains focused on exploration, cooperation, and the mystery behind the failing wards.

## Core cast and character voices

### The Apprentice — the player character
The player chooses a name and one of six arcane paths: Ember Mage, Frostweaver, Verdant Warden, Storm Herald, Voidcaller, or Sigilbinder. They begin with incomplete memories and a faint magical mark. Their voice and personality should remain player-defined; dialogue offers mystery and meaningful choices without forcing a fixed identity.

### Mathias, Battle Mage of the Council
Mathias is a veteran field-mage: practical, watchful, and quietly protective. He speaks in short, deliberate observations rather than grand speeches. He has spent decades maintaining the Council's wards and is unsettled by a corruption that seems to come from beyond the known realms. He knows the three seals, but not who has begun to unmake them. His defining flaw is that he withholds details until he believes an apprentice is ready—which can make him seem evasive.

### The Whispering Warden
An ancient presence shaped from blackwood, forgotten vows, and the last light of a damaged ward. The Warden is neither an ordinary enemy nor a trustworthy guide. It speaks in riddles that reveal themes rather than answers, remembers names removed from the Council's records, and warns that the three Sigils are promises as much as keys. Its first appearance hints at a hidden fourth force without naming the Hollow Crown too early.

### The Riftbound
Riftbound are creatures displaced and altered by a tear between realms. They move like shadows trying to remember their original shapes. They are dangerous because the corruption drives them, not because they are inherently evil; later chapters may reveal ways to calm, free, or understand them. Their visual language uses the shadow-beast model, violet rift light, and restrained silhouettes.

### The Hollow Crown
The long mystery behind the opening chapter. The Crown is not introduced as a simple monster; it is an erased claim to power, a missing name in the Council's history, and a force that can make records contradict themselves. Early dialogue should foreshadow it through omissions, altered memories, and gaps in the Codex rather than explain its full origin.
