# Arcane Realm — visual art direction

## North star
A polished, dimensional fantasy RPG with a higher-angle/isometric camera, atmospheric depth, expressive silhouettes, and a cohesive magical world. The experience should feel handcrafted, inviting, adventurous, and visually memorable—not pixel art, generic mobile-game clip-art, or a collection of unrelated asset packs.

## Camera and composition
- Design assets for the actual game camera and gameplay footprint, not just a beautiful standalone render.
- Keep a consistent world-space vertical axis, scale language, and perspective across characters, buildings, props, and terrain.
- Use clear foreground/midground/background separation and intentional overlap.
- Keep walkable paths, interactable objects, enemy silhouettes, and combat telegraphs visually legible.
- For UI/portrait art, define a separate framing convention rather than reusing world sprites blindly.

## Palette
- WoA identity: arcane purple and pink as signature accents, balanced with deep indigo, charcoal, moonlit neutrals, and warm highlights.
- Arcane: violet, lavender, silver, and luminous white.
- Ember: ember orange, molten gold, warm charcoal.
- Frost: icy cyan, glacial blue, silver, cool shadow.
- Verdant: emerald, jade, leaf green, warm earth.
- Shadow: plum, midnight blue, muted magenta, restrained spectral highlights.
- Regions may establish their own palettes. Avoid tinting every asset purple.

## Materials and lighting
Use material cues that remain readable at small scale: stone, aged timber, brass, glass, crystal, cloth, leather, foliage, water, and magical energy. Lighting should reinforce form and focus. Glows need a defined source and falloff; avoid uniform neon outlines and bloom that erases shape.

## Characters and traditions
- Preserve a consistent player body/rig and recognizable silhouette.
- Tradition identity should primarily use robe/appearance recoloring and accessories, not five unrelated body models.
- Keep clothing layers, staff scale, headwear, and equipment compatible with the camera.
- NPCs should be identifiable by silhouette, palette, props, and readable idle behavior.

## Environments
- Give each location a strong landmark and a readable navigational structure.
- Use modular kits where they preserve coherence, but avoid obvious copy-paste repetition.
- Set dressing should imply use and history while leaving gameplay space clear.
- Use animated lights, particles, foliage, water, and portal effects selectively.

## VFX and combat readability
- Every effect must communicate a gameplay purpose: cast, travel, impact, area, danger, defense, heal, status, or reward.
- Keep enemy windups and danger zones distinguishable from friendly effects.
- Use shape, timing, motion, and contrast—not color alone—to communicate.
- Provide low-cost and reduced-motion approaches when practical.

## UI language
- Dark, legible surfaces with restrained ornament, clear hierarchy, crisp icons, and consistent spacing.
- Purple/pink can highlight primary actions; other states need distinguishable treatment.
- Keep world visibility high and overlays concise.
- Design touch controls and safe-area margins for phones from the beginning.
- Avoid cursive body text, clutter, tiny labels, and oversized modal panels.

## Quality bar
At target gameplay scale, the asset must have a readable silhouette, intentional lighting, clean edges, coherent perspective, and a distinct purpose. It must look like it belongs to Arcane Realm even when viewed beside existing assets.
